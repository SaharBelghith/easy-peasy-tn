The Image of Who I Am

I want to be remembered as someone who saw life full of possibilities. I want to be remembered as someone who readily gave appreciation and support towards others. I want to be remembered as someone who planned a future out for herself and followed it. Making a clear path for myself will not be easy. Support from my parents and friends encourage me to keep on trying.

Being with my family gives me a feeling of safety. Having the opportunity to talk to family, when I need to, helps me to be open towards them. When I have a question to ask my parents, they are always willing to listen to what I have to say. When I do get in trouble, they do not yell at me, but tell me the consequences that might happen. Loving and caring for each other is what holds us together. A family is not a family without love.

Talking about my future and getting school accomplished, I would have to thank my parents for their support. My parents support me as I choose what I want to do with my future. « What do I have to do in order to become a pediatrician? » Without hesitation they tell me how much school I would have to go through. To believe in myself, I would have to be self-confident enough to achieve my goal.

Being around the people I care about has expanded my knowledge of life. My friends have taught me to value our friendship making sure they know I will always be there. My friends and I help each other out when we are stressed and having a difficult time.

Without my parents and friends I would be lost, they are the ones who guide me through life.

Whitney Church

Activity 2 — Pairwork

Jimmy's 8 years old. His mother died a few months ago. His father decided to get married again. Jimmy has got mixed feelings about that.

Guess what these feelings are.

Jimmy may feel sad and angry because he misses his mother; afraid that the new woman will take her place; jealous; but also a little happy and hopeful because his father will not be alone and someone will take care of the family. These are “mixed feelings”: good and bad at the same time.

📖 Reading — Lesson 2

The Day I Stopped Being a Stepmum

The Day I Stopped Being a Stepmum

Part One

From an early age I'd known I never wanted children of my own. It just happened when my Mr Right came along with a ready-made family.

Caption: Jennifer Middledich was always the stepmother then, on Mother's Day, came her well-deserved reward.

Suddenly, aged 20, I became stepmum to three children: Jay, nine, Dan, seven, and Kerry – the baby, aged five. At first, it was hard, to say the least. The children would do anything to test my patience.

I never tried to be anything other than a stepmum. The kids have their own mum and I've always respected that. I was always ‘Jenny’. As time passed however, it was wonderful to be accepted into their lives.

The older they grew, the fonder I grew of them, and the less I regretted never having children of my own. I loved them as if they were my own.

When Kerry announced she was having a baby, Steve and I were really happy. Kerry was like the daughter I'd never had.

So, when little Bradley arrived, I was knocked sideways by the outpouring of love I felt towards him. I've never been maternal but, holding him in my arms, I just melted.

Part Two

On Mother's Day last year, I was watching from the window, waiting for little Bradley, aged one to arrive.

“They're here, they're here,” I shrieked excitedly, as the car pulled up.

Eagerly, I ran out of the front door, and made straight for the car's rear door to get Bradley out of his baby seat.

“Hello B.J,” I said, as Bradley grinned up at me. I always called him B.J., short for Bradley Jones.

Clutched carefully in his little hand was a bunch of paper flowers.

“Give her the flowers,” Kerry urged gently.

He gave me the bouquet and sweetly said “Nan.”

I stared at him, astonished and in tears, then I gently took him out of his chair and held him to me tightly.

“I'm Bradley's Nan, I'm Bradley's Nan,” I kept repeating to myself with delight “he's just said so.”

Then, holding my precious flowers carefully, I went back inside with my grandson.

It was the happiest moment of my entire life. Since then I've become a grand-mother.

Bella, 09-03-1999

Present Perfect

Language Practice a

Focus on the following sentence taken from the last paragraph:

“Since then, I've become a grand-mother.”

1. Which tense is used?

2. What does it express?

3. What's the special indicator?

Language Practice b

Refer back to the text and find more sentences using the same tense.

Sentences from the text: “The kids have their own mum and I've always respected that.” · “I've never been maternal …” · “he's just said so.” · “Since then I've become a grand-mother.”

No, the indicators are not the same: always, never, just, since then. And the meaning is a little different each time: an action repeated up to now (always), an experience in her whole life (never), a very recent action (just), and a situation that started at one moment and continues now (since then).

Language Practice c

With your teacher, find out other uses of the same tense.

Other common uses: life experience (“I have never eaten fish and chips”), a past action with a result now (“I have lost my keys” → I can't get in), news that has just happened (“He has just arrived”), and something that has not happened up to now (“The film hasn't started yet”).

💡 Grammar Focus — The Present Perfect

Form

have / has + past participle

Examples from the lesson

  • I have become a grandmother. (I've become)
  • I have always respected that. (I've always respected)
  • I have never been maternal. (I've never been)
  • He has just said so. (He's just said)
  • My friends have taught me to value our friendship.

Structure

  • ➕ I / you / we / they have + past participle
  • ➕ he / she / it has + past participle
  • ➖ I have not (haven't) seen her.
  • ➖ She has not (hasn't) started yet.
  • ❓ Have you eaten fish and chips?

Main uses

  • 1. From the past until now. The action started in the past and is still true. → She has been unemployed since she left school.
  • 2. Life experience (we don't say when). → It's the first time I have eaten fish and chips.
  • 3. A past action with a result now. → I can't get into my house because I have lost my keys.
  • 4. Recent news / not finished time. → The film hasn't started yet.

Common indicators

since · for · yet · already · just · ever / never · always · it's the first time · so far · up to now

Present Perfect or Simple Past?

Present PerfectSimple Past
The time is not finished or not mentioned. The time is finished and often mentioned.
since, for, yet, ever, never, just, already, the first time yesterday, last year, in 1999, a few minutes ago, when I was a child
I haven't seen her since last year. They left a few minutes ago.

👉 Easy rule: with ago, yesterday, last … or when I was … → Simple Past. With since, yet, ever/never, just or first time → Present Perfect.

💬 Stage Three — Speaking

Give your opinion

Keeping in mind Jennifer's age when she got married to Mr Right, do you think she did the right thing?

💬 There is no single correct answer. Explain and justify your opinion. Then say it aloud to your partner.

Useful language: In my opinion … · I think … because … · On the one hand … on the other hand … · That's why …

The right thing: she loved Steve and the children; she gave them a home full of love; she became happy and never regretted it.
The wrong thing: she was only 20 — very young; she had never wanted children; three children is a big responsibility at that age.
